Aliya, please create a UNICEF-branded clickable mockup that shows how young people discover a career direction, build relevant capabilities and access earning opportunities. Potential.com connects vocational training, personalised career support and employer engagement in one continuous journey. Every participant should receive useful development support, while UNICEF can see progress and outcomes across training centres, partners and governorates.

Build a responsive front-end prototype using fictional data and simulated actions. Include the full-time employment, entrepreneurship and part-time or freelancing routes. Include all core modules and stakeholder views in this brief. Use shared components and role-specific data scope rather than disconnected designs.
No backend, real messages, real applications, payment flows, live AI integrations or production authentication are required for this mockup.
All people, employers, centres, vacancies, course content, session availability and statistics supplied below are fictional demonstration material unless explicitly identified as source evidence.

3 Career discovery assessment design
Y03 is a 12-question scenario assessment taking approximately 8–10 minutes. Each question has four options; users select the response most like them. Explain that the exercise supports exploration, has no pass or fail and does not determine admission or employment eligibility. Include a progress indicator, back, save and resume, and a final review screen.
Illustrative dimensions and scoring
Use four work-preference dimensions for the demo: P practical problem solving; C communication and collaboration; I initiative and enterprise; O organisation and delivery. Each option below awards two points to its indicated dimension. Scores are deterministic for repeatable demonstrations. These are mockup rules, not a scientific model or validated psychometric scoring system.
Question 1
A training project is falling behind. What do you do first?
P: Fix the task causing the delay.  |  C: Ask the group what support they need.  |  I: Suggest a new approach to the project.  |  O: Break the remaining work into a schedule.
Question 2
A local business wants to improve its service. What interests you most?
P: Finding a practical tool that solves the problem.  |  C: Talking to customers about their experience.  |  I: Designing a new service people would buy.  |  O: Improving the daily process and records.
Question 3
You are asked to help run a community event. Which task do you choose?
P: Set up equipment and troubleshoot.  |  C: Welcome participants and answer questions.  |  I: Create a new activity and attract interest.  |  O: Coordinate bookings and the timetable.
Question 4
You have a free afternoon to learn something. What do you choose?
P: Practise a hands-on technical skill.  |  C: Join a group discussion or presentation workshop.  |  I: Test an idea for a service.  |  O: Learn a tool for planning or handling data.
Question 5
A customer explains an unclear request. What is your first step?
P: Build a small example to check what works.  |  C: Ask questions and listen to their priorities.  |  I: Offer several creative possibilities.  |  O: Write down the requirements and constraints.
Question 6
Your group receives a small project budget. What do you enjoy doing?
P: Choose the tools and materials.  |  C: Agree responsibilities with the team.  |  I: Find a way to create extra value.  |  O: Track costs and deadlines.
4 Assessment questions and results
Question 7
You receive feedback that your work needs improvement. What helps you most?
P: A practical demonstration and another attempt.  |  C: A conversation to understand the feedback.  |  I: Freedom to try a different solution.  |  O: A clear checklist of improvements.
Question 8
A new opportunity has an unfamiliar task. What appeals to you?
P: Learning how to do the technical work.  |  C: Working with new people.  |  I: Exploring what the opportunity could become.  |  O: Understanding the process and delivering reliably.
Question 9
A team disagrees about a solution. What role do you naturally take?
P: Test the options against the actual problem.  |  C: Help people hear each other’s views.  |  I: Suggest a fresh option.  |  O: Compare options against agreed criteria.
Question 10
You are proudest after completing which task?
P: Repairing or creating something useful.  |  C: Helping a person or team succeed.  |  I: Turning an idea into an opportunity.  |  O: Delivering accurate work on time.
Question 11
A freelance client changes the request. What do you focus on first?
P: Check what is technically possible.  |  C: Clarify the client’s priorities.  |  I: Explore an improved solution.  |  O: Agree the revised scope and timeline.
Question 12
You are planning your next learning step. What draws your attention?
P: A technical skill I can practise.  |  C: A role involving service or teamwork.  |  I: An opportunity to create my own service.  |  O: A role involving coordination and reliable delivery.
Y04 Results and route choice
Show strongest two dimensions as a simple bar chart and a plain-language explanation. Examples: P plus O = Practical organiser; C plus O = People-focused coordinator; I plus C = Enterprising communicator. For ties, show “Balanced preferences” and let the user explore. Always show “These results are a starting point for exploration”.
Then ask three separate preference questions: preferred earning model, desired work environment and current priority. The earning-model answer drives the recommended route; the assessment dimensions inform suggested roles and learning. Do not infer that a personality score alone determines employment versus entrepreneurship.
Show all three route cards with Explore and Choose actions. Provide suggested career families and “Why this may suit you” explanations. Allow a user to choose any route, keep an optional secondary interest, edit the decision and retake the assessment. Retain completed learning when changing routes. Never present a match score as a hiring prediction.

7 Course catalogue career foundations
C01 Discover Your Career Direction
All routes • 35 minutes
Unit 1 — Know your strengths: Interpret interests and preferences without treating them as fixed labels.
Unit 2 — Explore ways of earning: Compare employment, entrepreneurship and freelancing using realistic trade-offs.
Unit 3 — Understand roles and skills: Read a sample opportunity and identify its required capabilities.
Unit 4 — Build your next step plan: Choose a goal, learning action and milestone for the next month.
Output: a three-step career action plan. Quiz example: A career profile should help you explore options, not decide your future permanently. Correct: True.
C02 Digital Essentials for Learning and Work
All routes • 40 minutes
Unit 1 — Use digital tools confidently: Organise files, create documents and join online learning.
Unit 2 — Communicate online: Write clear emails and use collaborative documents.
Unit 3 — Protect information: Recognise suspicious messages and share personal information carefully.
Unit 4 — Use AI thoughtfully: Check AI outputs and avoid uploading sensitive information.
Output: a professional email and organised sample folder structure. Quiz example: An unexpected request for your password should be independently verified, not answered. Correct: True.
C03 Communication and Teamwork at Work
All routes • 40 minutes
Unit 1 — Listen and ask clear questions: Confirm what another person needs before acting.
Unit 2 — Write with purpose: Create concise messages with a clear request and next step.
Unit 3 — Work with others: Allocate tasks and raise concerns respectfully.
Unit 4 — Handle difficult conversations: Respond calmly to feedback and resolve a service problem.
Output: a response to a dissatisfied customer. Quiz example: The first response to an unclear request is to ask clarifying questions. Correct: True.
C04 Work Habits and Personal Wellbeing
All routes • 30 minutes
Unit 1 — Plan your time: Break a goal into manageable tasks and priorities.
Unit 2 — Build dependable habits: Use checklists, calendars and progress reviews.
Unit 3 — Manage pressure: Identify practical ways to pause, ask for help and adjust workload.
Unit 4 — Set boundaries and seek support: Recognise when to contact a trainer or appropriate support service.
Output: a weekly learning and work plan. Keep this course educational; it must not offer clinical assessment or treatment.

Employment • 40 minutes

C07 Business Idea and Customer Discovery
Entrepreneurship • 45 minutes
Unit 1 — Choose a problem worth solving: Describe the customer and the problem before proposing a product.
Unit 2 — Talk to potential customers: Ask open questions about current behaviour rather than leading questions.
Unit 3 — Test a small solution: Create a simple service trial or prototype.
Unit 4 — Review the evidence: Decide what to keep, change or test next.
Output: problem statement and three customer interview questions. Sample scenario: Omar explores an appointment-booking service for local repair businesses; he first asks how they currently manage enquiries and missed appointments.
C08 Business Model and Money Basics
Entrepreneurship • 45 minutes
Unit 1 — Map how the business works: Identify customers, value, channels and key activities.
Unit 2 — Understand costs and pricing: Separate setup costs, regular costs and a proposed price.
Unit 3 — Plan simple cash flow: Estimate when cash comes in and when costs are paid.
Unit 4 — Check assumptions: Compare an optimistic and cautious example before committing resources.
Output: one-page business model and sample cash plan. Use fictional amounts only; show no financing promise or business-registration advice.
